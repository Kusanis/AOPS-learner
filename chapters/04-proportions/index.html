Chapter 4 · Algebra & Number

Proportions

Twice the ingredients make twice the cookies; twice the workers finish in half the time. Proportions capture these "scale it up" relationships in a single equation. In this chapter you'll set up direct and inverse variation, juggle ratios, convert units without guessing, and handle percents, including the ones that trick almost everybody.

Each example climbs from Warm-up to Challenge. Press N for the next step, F for the full solution.
Key methods in this chapter
  • Direct proportion: the quotient is constant, $\dfrac{y}{x} = k$. Inverse proportion: the product is constant, $xy = k$.
  • Joint and combined variation: put directly proportional quantities on the opposite side of the fraction from $x$, inversely proportional ones on the same side, and find the one constant.
  • A ratio $p : q$ splits a whole into $p + q$ equal parts.
  • If $\dfrac ab = \dfrac cd$, then $\dfrac ab = \dfrac{a + kc}{b + kd}$ for any $k$ (when the denominator isn't $0$). Multiply ratios to cancel a shared quantity.
  • Conversion factors are fractions equal to $1$; choose the orientation that cancels the unit you want to remove, and repeat it for squared or cubed units.
  • Percent means "per hundred". "Of" means multiply; an increase of $p\%$ multiplies by $1 + \tfrac{p}{100}$, a decrease by $1 - \tfrac{p}{100}$.

4.1 Direct and Inverse

Hook

A recipe for 4 people needs 300 g of pasta. Cooking for 10? Scale everything by $\tfrac{10}{4}$. Now a different question: a pot of soup feeds 4 people for 3 days. How long would it feed 6 people? More people means fewer days. Both are proportions, but they scale in opposite directions.

Concept

A ratio compares two quantities by division. "The ratio of apples to oranges is $3 : 2$" means $\dfrac{\text{apples}}{\text{oranges}} = \dfrac32$; out of every $5$ pieces of fruit, $3$ are apples. Ratios of three or more quantities use colons: ages $30 : 12 : 6$ simplify to $5 : 2 : 1$ by dividing by $6$.

Direct proportion: $\dfrac{y}{x} = k$ (constant). When $x$ doubles, $y$ doubles.

Inverse proportion: $xy = k$ (constant). When $x$ doubles, $y$ halves.

The constant $k$ is the constant of proportionality. To solve a problem, find $k$ from the information given, then use it for the new situation.

A quantity can depend on several others at once. If $z$ is directly proportional to both $x$ and $y$ (jointly proportional) and inversely proportional to $w$, then

$$\frac{zw}{xy} = k \qquad\text{or}\qquad z = \frac{kxy}{w}.$$

To decide which kind of proportion you have, ask: "If this goes up while everything else stays fixed, does that go up or down?"

Scale a price

Six pencils cost 90 cents. At the same rate, how much do 14 pencils cost?

  1. $\dfrac{\text{cost}}{\text{pencils}} = \dfrac{90}{6} = 15$ cents per pencil.

    Cost is directly proportional to the number of pencils, so the quotient is constant.

  2. $\text{cost} = 15 \times 14 = 210$ cents.

    Use the same constant for the new number of pencils.

210 cents (2.10 dollars)

Split by a ratio

A bag of 64 marbles has red and blue marbles in the ratio $5 : 3$. How many of each?

  1. The ratio splits the marbles into $5 + 3 = 8$ equal parts of $\dfrac{64}{8} = 8$ marbles.

    For every 5 red there are 3 blue, so every group of 8 marbles has the same mix.

  2. Red: $5 \times 8 = 40$. Blue: $3 \times 8 = 24$.

    Take 5 parts and 3 parts. Check: $40 + 24 = 64$.

40 red and 24 blue

More workers, fewer days

Six workers can build a wall in 15 days. How long would 9 workers take, working at the same rate?

  1. More workers means fewer days, so workers and days are inversely proportional: $(\text{workers})(\text{days}) = 6 \times 15 = 90$.

    The product is the total "worker-days" of labour the wall needs, which doesn't change.

  2. $9 \times \text{days} = 90$, so $\text{days} = 10$.

    Same constant, new number of workers.

10 days

Chain two ratios

If $a : b = 2 : 3$ and $b : c = 4 : 5$, find $a : b : c$.

  1. Make $b$ the same in both: $2 : 3 = 8 : 12$ and $4 : 5 = 12 : 15$.

    $b$ is 3 parts in one ratio and 4 in the other; scale both to $\operatorname{lcm}(3, 4) = 12$ parts.

  2. So $a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15$.

    With a common value for $b$, the ratios link up.

$8 : 12 : 15$

Inverse with a square

$y$ is inversely proportional to the square of $x$, and $y = 8$ when $x = 3$. Find $y$ when $x = 6$.

  1. $x^2 y = k$, so $k = 3^2 \cdot 8 = 72$.

    "Inversely proportional to $x^2$" means the product $x^2 y$ is constant.

  2. $6^2 y = 72$, so $y = 2$.

    Doubling $x$ multiplies $x^2$ by 4, so $y$ is divided by 4.

$y = 2$

Joint and inverse together

$z$ varies jointly with $x$ and $y$ and inversely with $w$. If $z = 12$ when $x = 2$, $y = 3$, $w = 4$, find $z$ when $x = 5$, $y = 6$, $w = 10$.

  1. $\dfrac{zw}{xy} = k$, so $k = \dfrac{12 \cdot 4}{2 \cdot 3} = 8$.

    $x$ and $y$ go opposite $z$ in the fraction (direct); $w$ goes on the same side as $z$ (inverse).

  2. $z = \dfrac{kxy}{w} = \dfrac{8 \cdot 5 \cdot 6}{10} = 24$.

    Solve for $z$ and use the new values.

$z = 24$

Machines, parts and hours

Six machines make 240 parts in 4 hours. How many hours do 9 machines need to make 450 parts?

  1. Parts are directly proportional to machines and to hours: $\dfrac{\text{parts}}{(\text{machines})(\text{hours})} = k$.

    More machines or more hours both mean more parts.

  2. $k = \dfrac{240}{6 \cdot 4} = 10$ parts per machine-hour.

    Find the constant from the first situation.

  3. $\text{hours} = \dfrac{450}{10 \cdot 9} = 5$.

    Rearrange for hours and substitute.

5 hours

What happens to z?

$z = \dfrac{kx^2}{y}$. If $x$ doubles and $y$ triples, by what factor does $z$ change?

  1. New $z = \dfrac{k(2x)^2}{3y} = \dfrac{4kx^2}{3y}$.

    Substitute $2x$ and $3y$ for the old values.

  2. $= \dfrac43 \cdot \dfrac{kx^2}{y} = \dfrac43 z$.

    Pull out the original expression; what's left is the factor.

$z$ is multiplied by $\dfrac43$.

You don't need $k$ to find how a proportional quantity scales.

Clock hands at a right angle

At exactly 2:00, how many minutes pass before the hour and minute hands first form a right angle?

  1. Measure positions in "minute marks" (60 around the dial). After $x$ minutes the minute hand is at mark $x$.

    Using one unit for both hands lets us compare them directly.

  2. The hour hand starts at mark $10$ and moves $5$ marks per $60$ minutes, so it is at $10 + \dfrac{x}{12}$.

    Its movement is directly proportional to time: $\tfrac{5}{60} = \tfrac{1}{12}$ mark per minute.

  3. A right angle is $15$ marks. The minute hand first gets $15$ marks ahead: $x = 10 + \dfrac{x}{12} + 15$.

    At 2:00 the minute hand is 10 marks behind, so it must catch up and pull 15 ahead.

  4. $\dfrac{11x}{12} = 25$, so $x = \dfrac{300}{11} = 27\tfrac{3}{11}$ minutes.

    Solve the linear equation.

$\dfrac{300}{11} \approx 27.27$ minutes

In clock problems, both hands move: write each position as a function of time.

4.2 Manipulating Proportions

Hook

One glass of juice is 1 part syrup to 3 parts water. A second, bigger glass is 2 parts syrup to 6 parts water: the same strength. Pour them together and you get 3 parts syrup to 9 parts water, which is still the same strength. Adding tops and adding bottoms of equal ratios keeps the ratio. That small fact is surprisingly powerful.

Concept

Suppose $\dfrac ab = \dfrac cd$. Call the common value $r$, so $a = rb$ and $c = rd$. Then for any number $k$,

$$\frac{a + kc}{b + kd} = \frac{rb + krd}{b + kd} = \frac{r(b + kd)}{b + kd} = r. \qquad\text{So}\quad \frac ab = \frac cd = \frac{a+c}{b+d} = \frac{a-c}{b-d} = \frac{a+kc}{b+kd}$$

(as long as the new denominator isn't zero). Naming the common ratio $r$ (or $t$) is itself a great technique: if $\dfrac x3 = \dfrac y4$, write $x = 3t$, $y = 4t$.

Ratios also multiply to cancel a shared quantity: if $\dfrac xy = \dfrac45$ and $\dfrac yz = \dfrac53$, then $\dfrac xz = \dfrac xy \cdot \dfrac yz = \dfrac43$.

Most ratio equations still yield to plain algebra: clear denominators by cross-multiplying, collect terms, and divide to get the ratio you want.

Add one to a ratio

If $\dfrac ab = \dfrac35$, find $\dfrac{a + b}{b}$.

  1. $\dfrac{a+b}{b} = \dfrac ab + \dfrac bb = \dfrac ab + 1$.

    Split the fraction into two pieces with the same denominator.

  2. $= \dfrac35 + 1 = \dfrac85$.

    Substitute the known ratio.

$\dfrac85$

Multiply ratios to cancel

If $\dfrac xy = \dfrac23$ and $\dfrac yz = \dfrac67$, find $\dfrac xz$.

  1. $\dfrac xz = \dfrac xy \cdot \dfrac yz$.

    The $y$ on top of the second ratio cancels the $y$ on the bottom of the first.

  2. $= \dfrac23 \cdot \dfrac67 = \dfrac{12}{21} = \dfrac47$.

    Multiply and reduce.

$\dfrac47$

Solve for a ratio

If $\dfrac{x + y}{x - y} = 5$, find $\dfrac xy$.

  1. Multiply by $x - y$: $x + y = 5x - 5y$.

    Clear the denominator.

  2. $6y = 4x$.

    Collect $x$ terms on one side and $y$ terms on the other.

  3. Divide both sides by $4y$: $\dfrac xy = \dfrac64 = \dfrac32$.

    We want $x$ over $y$, so divide by $y$ (and by $x$'s coefficient).

$\dfrac32$

A messier ratio equation

If $\dfrac{3x - y}{x + 2y} = \dfrac45$, find $\dfrac xy$.

  1. Cross-multiply: $5(3x - y) = 4(x + 2y)$, so $15x - 5y = 4x + 8y$.

    Equal fractions have equal cross products.

  2. $11x = 13y$, so $\dfrac xy = \dfrac{13}{11}$.

    Group like terms, then divide by $11y$.

$\dfrac{13}{11}$

Combine equal ratios

If $\dfrac ab = \dfrac cd = \dfrac27$, find $\dfrac{3a - 2c}{3b - 2d}$ (assume the denominator isn't zero).

  1. Write $a = \tfrac27 b$ and $c = \tfrac27 d$.

    Name the common ratio and express the tops in terms of the bottoms.

  2. $3a - 2c = \tfrac27(3b - 2d)$, so the fraction equals $\tfrac27$.

    The factor $\tfrac27$ comes out of every term, and $3b - 2d$ cancels.

$\dfrac27$

Any combination $\dfrac{ma + nc}{mb + nd}$ of equal ratios has the same value.

Prove the addition property

Show that if $\dfrac ab = \dfrac cd$ and $b + d \ne 0$, then $\dfrac{a + c}{b + d} = \dfrac ab$.

  1. Cross-multiplying the given ratio: $ad = bc$.

    Turn the proportion into an equation without fractions.

  2. We want $(a + c)b = a(b + d)$, i.e. $ab + bc = ab + ad$.

    Cross-multiplying the claimed equation shows what must be proved.

  3. That reduces to $bc = ad$, which is exactly what we know. ∎

    Every step is reversible, so the claim follows from the hypothesis.

Both reduce to $ad = bc$, so the property holds.

Three equal ratios

If $\dfrac x3 = \dfrac y4 = \dfrac z5$ and $x + y + z = 36$, find $x$, $y$ and $z$.

  1. Let the common value be $t$: $x = 3t$, $y = 4t$, $z = 5t$.

    One letter now describes all three unknowns.

  2. $3t + 4t + 5t = 12t = 36$, so $t = 3$.

    Use the sum condition. (Equivalently, the addition property gives $t = \tfrac{36}{3 + 4 + 5}$.)

  3. $x = 9$, $y = 12$, $z = 15$.

    Substitute back.

$(x, y, z) = (9, 12, 15)$

Evaluate using a ratio

If $a : b = 3 : 4$ (with $b \ne 0$), find $\dfrac{2a + 5b}{4a - b}$.

  1. Write $a = 3t$, $b = 4t$ with $t \ne 0$.

    Only the ratio is known, so introduce a scale factor.

  2. $\dfrac{6t + 20t}{12t - 4t} = \dfrac{26t}{8t} = \dfrac{13}{4}$.

    Every term has a factor of $t$, which cancels. That's why the ratio alone determines the answer.

$\dfrac{13}{4}$

A ratio of squares

If $\dfrac ab = \dfrac23$, find $\dfrac{a^2 + b^2}{ab}$.

  1. $\dfrac{a^2 + b^2}{ab} = \dfrac{a^2}{ab} + \dfrac{b^2}{ab} = \dfrac ab + \dfrac ba$.

    Splitting the fraction reveals the ratio and its reciprocal.

  2. $= \dfrac23 + \dfrac32 = \dfrac{4 + 9}{6} = \dfrac{13}{6}$.

    Substitute and add.

$\dfrac{13}{6}$

A symmetric proportion

Positive numbers $a$, $b$, $c$ satisfy $\dfrac{a}{b + c} = \dfrac{b}{c + a} = \dfrac{c}{a + b} = k$. Find $k$.

  1. By the addition property, $k = \dfrac{a + b + c}{(b + c) + (c + a) + (a + b)}$.

    Adding all the tops and all the bottoms of equal ratios gives the same ratio.

  2. $= \dfrac{a + b + c}{2(a + b + c)} = \dfrac12$.

    The denominator is twice the numerator, and $a + b + c \ne 0$ since all are positive.

$k = \dfrac12$

When ratios are equal, adding tops and bottoms often exposes hidden symmetry.

4.3 Conversion Factors

Hook

A runner finishes 5 kilometers. How many miles is that? Do you multiply by 1.609 or divide by it? Guessing gives a 50/50 chance of nonsense. Conversion factors remove the guessing: you let the units tell you what to do.

Concept

Start with a fact like $1 \text{ mi} = 1.609 \text{ km}$. Dividing both sides by one side gives two fractions that each equal $1$:

$$\frac{1.609\ \text{km}}{1\ \text{mi}} = 1 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \frac{1\ \text{mi}}{1.609\ \text{km}} = 1.$$

Multiplying by $1$ never changes a quantity, so you can multiply by either. Choose the one that puts the unit you want to get rid of on the opposite side, so it cancels:

$$5\ \text{km} \times \frac{1\ \text{mi}}{1.609\ \text{km}} \approx 3.11\ \text{mi}.$$

Chain as many factors as you need, cancelling one unit at a time. Units in denominators ("per hour", "per gallon") need a factor with that unit on top.

Squared and cubed units. $\text{ft}^2$ means $\text{ft}\cdot\text{ft}$, so it takes the conversion factor twice: $1\ \text{ft}^2 = 1\ \text{ft}^2 \times \left(\dfrac{12\ \text{in}}{1\ \text{ft}}\right)^2 = 144\ \text{in}^2$. Cubed units take it three times.

Hours to seconds

Convert 3.5 hours to minutes and to seconds.

  1. $3.5\ \text{h} \times \dfrac{60\ \text{min}}{1\ \text{h}} = 210\ \text{min}$.

    "h" sits on the bottom of the factor so that it cancels the "h" we start with.

  2. $210\ \text{min} \times \dfrac{60\ \text{s}}{1\ \text{min}} = 12600\ \text{s}$.

    A second factor cancels minutes and leaves seconds.

210 minutes, 12600 seconds

Miles per hour to feet per second

Convert 60 mi/h to ft/s (1 mi = 5280 ft).

  1. $\dfrac{60\ \text{mi}}{1\ \text{h}} \times \dfrac{5280\ \text{ft}}{1\ \text{mi}} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{h}}{3600\ \text{s}}$.

    Miles are on top, so the first factor has miles on the bottom; hours are on the bottom, so the second factor has hours on top.

  2. $= \dfrac{60 \cdot 5280}{3600}\ \dfrac{\text{ft}}{\text{s}} = 88\ \text{ft/s}$.

    Miles and hours cancel, leaving feet per second.

88 ft/s

Kilometers to miles

How many miles is a 5 km race? Use 1 mi ≈ 1.609 km.

  1. $5\ \text{km} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{mi}}{1.609\ \text{km}}$.

    We start with km, so km must be on the bottom of the factor. That settles "multiply or divide": we divide by $1.609$.

  2. $\approx 3.11\ \text{mi}$.

    Sanity check: a mile is longer than a kilometer, so the number of miles should be smaller.

about 3.11 miles

Square units

Convert 2 square yards to square feet, and 1 square meter to square centimeters.

  1. $2\ \text{yd}^2 \times \left(\dfrac{3\ \text{ft}}{1\ \text{yd}}\right)^2 = 2 \times 9\ \text{ft}^2 = 18\ \text{ft}^2$.

    $\text{yd}^2 = \text{yd}\cdot\text{yd}$, so the factor is needed twice to cancel both.

  2. $1\ \text{m}^2 \times \left(\dfrac{100\ \text{cm}}{1\ \text{m}}\right)^2 = 10000\ \text{cm}^2$.

    A 1 m square is 100 cm by 100 cm, which is $10000$ little squares, not $100$.

18 ft² and 10000 cm²

Made-up currencies

If 3 zips are worth 5 zaps and 2 zaps are worth 7 zoops, how many zoops are 12 zips worth?

  1. $12\ \text{zips} \times \dfrac{5\ \text{zaps}}{3\ \text{zips}} = 20\ \text{zaps}$.

    There's no direct zip-to-zoop rate, so go through zaps.

  2. $20\ \text{zaps} \times \dfrac{7\ \text{zoops}}{2\ \text{zaps}} = 70\ \text{zoops}$.

    The second factor cancels zaps.

70 zoops

A leaky faucet

A faucet drips 1 mL every 2 seconds. How many liters does it waste per day?

  1. $\dfrac{1\ \text{mL}}{2\ \text{s}} \times \dfrac{60\ \text{s}}{1\ \text{min}} \times \dfrac{60\ \text{min}}{1\ \text{h}} \times \dfrac{24\ \text{h}}{1\ \text{day}}$.

    Seconds are on the bottom, so we chain factors that carry the bottom unit up to days.

  2. $= \dfrac{86400}{2}\ \dfrac{\text{mL}}{\text{day}} = 43200\ \text{mL/day}$.

    $60 \cdot 60 \cdot 24 = 86400$ seconds in a day.

  3. $43200\ \text{mL} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{L}}{1000\ \text{mL}} = 43.2\ \text{L}$.

    Convert the top unit too.

43.2 liters per day

Density in different units

Water has density $1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$. Express this in $\text{g/cm}^3$.

  1. Top: $1000\ \text{kg} \times \dfrac{1000\ \text{g}}{1\ \text{kg}} = 10^6\ \text{g}$.

    Convert the numerator's unit.

  2. Bottom: $\dfrac{1}{\text{m}^3} \times \left(\dfrac{1\ \text{m}}{100\ \text{cm}}\right)^3 = \dfrac{1}{10^6\ \text{cm}^3}$.

    $\text{m}^3$ is in the denominator, so the factor needs m on top, cubed.

  3. $\dfrac{10^6\ \text{g}}{10^6\ \text{cm}^3} = 1\ \text{g/cm}^3$.

    The two powers of ten cancel.

$1\ \text{g/cm}^3$

Cubic feet to liters

How many liters are in one cubic foot? Use 1 in = 2.54 cm and 1 L = 1000 cm³.

  1. $1\ \text{ft}^3 \times \left(\dfrac{12\ \text{in}}{1\ \text{ft}}\right)^3 = 1728\ \text{in}^3$.

    Cube the factor because the unit is cubed.

  2. $1728\ \text{in}^3 \times \left(\dfrac{2.54\ \text{cm}}{1\ \text{in}}\right)^3 \approx 1728 \times 16.387\ \text{cm}^3 \approx 28317\ \text{cm}^3$.

    Again cube the factor: $2.54^3 \approx 16.387$.

  3. $28317\ \text{cm}^3 \times \dfrac{1\ \text{L}}{1000\ \text{cm}^3} \approx 28.3\ \text{L}$.

    Finish with the liter conversion.

about 28.3 liters

Fuel economy, flipped

A car gets 30 miles per gallon. Express this in liters per 100 km. (1 mi ≈ 1.609 km, 1 gal ≈ 3.785 L.)

  1. $\dfrac{30\ \text{mi}}{1\ \text{gal}} \times \dfrac{1.609\ \text{km}}{1\ \text{mi}} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{gal}}{3.785\ \text{L}} \approx 12.75\ \dfrac{\text{km}}{\text{L}}$.

    Convert both the top and bottom units.

  2. Flip: $\dfrac{1\ \text{L}}{12.75\ \text{km}}$.

    "Liters per km" is the reciprocal of "km per liter". The units tell you to take the reciprocal.

  3. $\dfrac{1\ \text{L}}{12.75\ \text{km}} \times 100\ \text{km} \approx 7.84\ \text{L}$, so about 7.8 L per 100 km.

    Multiply by 100 km to get the fuel used over 100 km.

about 7.8 L per 100 km

When a rate is "upside down" from what you want, take its reciprocal; the units show you when.

4.4 Percent

Hook

A shop raises a price by 50%, then puts it "50% off". Back to where it started? No: you now pay only 75% of the original price. Percent changes multiply, they don't add, and the base changes after every step.

Concept

Percent means "per hundred": $p\% = \dfrac{p}{100}$. So $35\% = 0.35 = \tfrac{7}{20}$, and $\tfrac38 = 0.375 = 37.5\%$.

Translate words into algebra: "of" means multiply, and "is" means equals. "What percent of 40 is 14?" becomes $\dfrac{p}{100}\cdot 40 = 14$.

Increase by $p\%$: multiply by $\left(1 + \dfrac{p}{100}\right)$.   Decrease by $p\%$: multiply by $\left(1 - \dfrac{p}{100}\right)$.

$$\text{percent change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100\%$$

Thinking in multipliers makes repeated changes easy. Interest of $r\%$ per year for $n$ years multiplies an amount by $\left(1 + \tfrac{r}{100}\right)^n$. To undo a change, divide by its multiplier.

In mixtures, the concentration is (amount of the ingredient) ÷ (total amount). Track the ingredient itself: it adds up, while percents do not.

Convert between forms

Write $12.5\%$ as a fraction and a decimal, and write $\tfrac38$ as a percent.

  1. $12.5\% = \dfrac{12.5}{100} = 0.125 = \dfrac{125}{1000} = \dfrac18$.

    Divide by 100, then reduce the fraction.

  2. $\dfrac38 = 0.375 = 37.5\%$.

    To go from a decimal to a percent, multiply by 100.

$12.5\% = 0.125 = \tfrac18$; $\ \tfrac38 = 37.5\%$

A percent of a number

What is 15% of 80?

  1. $0.15 \times 80 = 12$.

    "Of" means multiply, and $15\% = 0.15$.

$12$

Find the whole

18 is 24% of what number?

  1. Let the number be $n$: $18 = 0.24n$.

    "Is" means equals and "of" means multiply.

  2. $n = \dfrac{18}{0.24} = 75$.

    Divide to isolate $n$. Check: $0.24 \times 75 = 18$.

$75$

Find the percent

What percent of 40 is 14?

  1. $\dfrac{p}{100} \cdot 40 = 14$, so $\dfrac{p}{100} = \dfrac{14}{40} = 0.35$.

    The unknown is the percent itself.

  2. $p = 35$.

    Multiply by 100.

$35\%$

Discount, then tax

A jacket costs 60 dollars. It is marked 15% off, and then 8% sales tax is added. What is the final price?

  1. After the discount: $60 \times 0.85 = 51$ dollars.

    Taking off 15% leaves 85% of the price.

  2. With tax: $51 \times 1.08 = 55.08$ dollars.

    Adding 8% means multiplying by $1.08$. In one line: $60 \times 0.85 \times 1.08$.

55.08 dollars

Percent change goes both ways

A price rises from 80 to 92 dollars. What is the percent increase? If it later falls from 92 back to 80, what is the percent decrease?

  1. Increase: $\dfrac{92 - 80}{80} = \dfrac{12}{80} = 0.15 = 15\%$.

    Percent change is measured against the starting value, here 80.

  2. Decrease: $\dfrac{92 - 80}{92} = \dfrac{12}{92} \approx 0.1304 \approx 13.0\%$.

    Same 12-dollar change, but now the starting value is 92, so the percent is smaller.

15% increase; about 13.0% decrease

Compound interest

5000 dollars earns 4% interest per year, compounded yearly. How much is there after 3 years?

  1. Each year multiplies the balance by $1.04$.

    Interest is added to the current balance, so each year's 4% is 4% of a bigger amount.

  2. $5000 \times 1.04^3 = 5000 \times 1.124864 = 5624.32$.

    Three years means three factors of $1.04$.

5624.32 dollars

Undo a raise

After a 20% raise, a salary is 54000 dollars. What was it before?

  1. Old salary $\times 1.2 = 54000$.

    The raise multiplied the old salary by $1.2$.

  2. Old salary $= \dfrac{54000}{1.2} = 45000$.

    Undo a multiplier by dividing. (Subtracting 20% of 54000 would give 43200, which is wrong: the 20% was of the old salary.)

45000 dollars

Diluting a solution

How many liters of pure water must be added to 6 L of a 30% salt solution to make a 20% solution?

  1. Salt present: $0.30 \times 6 = 1.8$ L.

    Track the ingredient. Adding water doesn't change the amount of salt.

  2. With $x$ liters of water added, the concentration is $\dfrac{1.8}{6 + x} = 0.20$.

    Concentration = salt ÷ total volume.

  3. $1.8 = 0.2(6 + x)$, so $6 + x = 9$ and $x = 3$.

    Clear the denominator and solve.

3 liters

Getting back to the start

A town's population grows by 60% in one year. By what percent must it then shrink to return to its original size?

  1. Let the shrink be $p\%$, with multiplier $m = 1 - \tfrac{p}{100}$. We need $1.6\,m = 1$.

    Two multipliers in a row must cancel out to 1.

  2. $m = \dfrac{1}{1.6} = 0.625$, so $p = 37.5$.

    The decrease is taken from the larger, grown population, so a smaller percent is enough.

  3. Check with 1000 people: $1000 \to 1600 \to 1600 \times 0.625 = 1000$. ✓

    Testing with a concrete number confirms the result.

37.5%

To undo a change with multiplier $m$, you need multiplier $\dfrac1m$, not the same percent in reverse.
